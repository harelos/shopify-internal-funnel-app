# The Nova PDP copy formula

Derived from three things and nothing else: the house PDP CRO reference, the
Hebrew persuasion reference, and what the leading beauty PDPs actually do.

The problem it fixes: the first pass wrote **descriptions**. Accurate, readable,
and they sold nothing, because every section was introduced by a label
(`איך משתמשים`, `נפח`) rather than by a claim, and the reader had no reason to
get from one sentence to the next.

---

## The two tests every product must pass

**1. The scanner test.** Delete every body paragraph. Read only: eyebrow, H1,
promise, chips, section headings, price, CTA. A woman who reads only that must
still be able to answer: what is it, is it for me, why would it work, what do I
pay, what do I do now. If she can't, the scanner path is broken and most of the
traffic sees a broken page.

**2. The competitor-swap test.** Put a rival brand's name on the promise line.
If it still reads fine, the line is generic. Rewrite it.

---

## The slippery slide

Each sentence has one job: make the next sentence feel natural to read. Not to
explain the product, not to prove everything, not to close. So the first
sentence of every description is **a moment she recognises**, never the product.

Ask of every line: after this, how likely is she to want the next one?

---

## The fields, and the formula for each

### 1. Eyebrow
`NovaHair` for hair, `NovaGlow` for skin. Never anything else.

### 2. H1 — under 24 Hebrew characters
The product, named plainly. One line at 390px. The H1 is not where the selling
happens.

### 3. Promise (`nova.subtitle`) — the most important line on the page
> **what visibly changes + the exact moment it matters**

Not a feature. Not a category benefit. It must fail the competitor-swap test.

| weak | why | strong |
|---|---|---|
| נוסחה עשירה ללחות | could sit on any bottle | מכסה את השורש שיצא, עד החפיפה הבאה |
| טיפוח מתקדם לשיער | label, not a claim | עשר דקות בשבוע, במקום תור במספרה |
| מחזק את השיער | vague, unprovable | עובד על הקרקפת, לא על האורך |

### 4. Chips (`nova.chips`) — three, two to four words each
The scanner's whole diet. Concrete outcomes, never adjectives.
`בלי קערה` · `עד החפיפה` · `גם על שיער צבוע`

### 5. Lead (first paragraph of the description)
Three sentences, in this order:
1. a moment she recognises, with a real detail in it
2. the friction inside that moment
3. the turn, which is where the product first appears

> השורש יוצא אחרי שלושה שבועות, התור במספרה בעוד שלושה נוספים. באמצע את עומדת
> מול המראה ומחליטה אם שווה להתחיל את כל הסיפור של קערה ומברשת בשביל רצועה אחת.
> השפופרת הזאת נותנת תשובה אחרת.

### 6. Mechanism (`nova.mechanism`) — `claim heading | body`
The heading must be **a claim she could argue with**, never a label.
The body runs cause → action → outcome in two or three sentences.

| label (banned) | claim (use) |
|---|---|
| איך זה עובד | הצבע יושב על השורש, לא על האורך |
| המנגנון | הקרקפת סופגת בעשר דקות, השערה לא |
| היתרונות שלנו | מוצק, כי בקבוק הוא רוב המשקל ורוב המחיר |

### 7. Steps (`nova.steps`) — three, `title | text`
Imperative, numbered, each with one specific detail: a time, a quantity, a
place. The purpose is not instruction, it is letting her picture owning it.

### 8. What to expect (`nova.expect`) — three, `when | what`
Calibrated and never guaranteed. This block exists because beauty products fail
in the customer's head at week two, and saying in advance what is normal
prevents that. It also lowers returns.

> `בשימוש הראשון | הגוון תופס חלקית. זה תקין, שיער לבן סופג לאט.`

### 9. Fit (`nova.fit_yes`, `nova.fit_no`)
Three reasons it suits her, and **two honest reasons it might not**. The second
list is the cheapest trust the page can buy, and it stops the wrong customer
ordering and then returning.

### 10. FAQ (`nova.faq`) — three, `question | answer`
The question must be in her words, phrased as she would type it, not as a
support-desk category. `ומה אם השיער כבר צבוע?` not `התאמה לשיער צבוע`.

---

## Rules that override everything above

- **No em dash or en dash in customer copy.** The hyphen in a product title is a
  delimiter the template consumes; the customer never sees it.
- **Female singular**, Israeli Hebrew conceived in Hebrew, not translated.
- **No invented proof.** No review counts, no statistics, no clinical claims, no
  customer numbers, no local warehouse, no scarcity. Where proof is thin, narrow
  the claim instead of inflating it.
- **No medical claims.** These are cosmetics. Hair loss that is sudden or patchy
  gets pointed at a dermatologist, not at a bottle.
- A rhetorical question is allowed once per page, and never two in a row.

## What was taken from where

**Ulta / Sephora.** The summary formula: *brand + product + verb + outcome,
then "crafted with X, Y and Z, the formula does A and B."* Also the
cross-sell module that carries a real price and a single add button, which is
now the cart drawer's `משלים את הטיפול` block.

**The house PDP CRO reference.** Proof-first archetype for beauty. Section order
by buyer question. Mechanism before comparison. Timeline to set expectations.
One dominant psychological job per section.

**The house Hebrew reference.** The slippery slide, the double readership path,
subheads that carry claims, and the rule against advertising the category
instead of the product.

**Deliberately not taken.** Fake scarcity bars, countdown timers, invented
percentages, decorative experts, before-and-after photos we do not own, and
review walls. Borrow the structure, not the liability.
