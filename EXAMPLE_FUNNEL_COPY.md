# Example Funnel Copy: 99 Jerseys Custom Matchday Shirt

Status: example copy for the local preview only.

This document deliberately separates usable copy from facts that still need
owner verification. The product, price, delivery terms, reviews, guarantee,
team licensing, fabric specifications, and stock claims below are placeholders
unless marked as verified. Replace bracketed fields before any customer-facing
publication.

## Funnel promise and audience

**Audience:** A football supporter who wants to wear something personal on
matchday, at a watch party, or while giving a meaningful gift. They are not
looking for another disposable piece of fan merchandise. They want the moment
when the shirt arrives, the name is on the back, and it feels like it belongs
to them.

**One big idea:** A shirt becomes more meaningful when it carries the name and
number that make the matchday memory personal.

**Primary action:** Choose a shirt and continue to the native Shopify checkout.

**Facts still required:** final product name, available sizes, personalization
rules, price, shipping times, return policy, official club/licensing status,
materials, review count, payment methods, and guarantee. No unsupported
"official," "best-selling," "limited," delivery, or review claim is used as a
fact in this example.

## Experiment map

### Experiment 1: Pre-sell angle

- **Step:** Pre-sell / advertorial.
- **Variant A:** Personal story advertorial.
- **Variant B:** Seven-reasons listicle.
- **Allocation:** Configurable basis points, example 5,000 / 5,000.
- **Primary metric:** Next-step entry to the sales page.
- **Guardrails:** page load, CTA click rate, checkout-start rate, and any
  complaint or refund signal once live data exists.

### Experiment 2: Sales-page angle

- **Step:** Sales page.
- **Variant A:** Story and proof-led page.
- **Variant B:** Offer and value-led page.
- **Allocation:** Configurable basis points, example 5,000 / 5,000.
- **Primary metric:** Native Shopify checkout started per sales-page visitor.
- **Guardrails:** paid-order rate, revenue per visitor, refund/cancellation
  rate, and attribution coverage.

Shopify Basic checkout is the measurement boundary. The checkout itself is not
split-tested or structurally customized in this funnel.

## Step 1, Variant A: Advertorial

### Job in the journey

Turn a cold click into personal recognition. The reader should feel that the
shirt is connected to a real matchday moment before they see an offer.

### Visual job

Use one intimate, documentary-style image: a supporter holding the shirt just
before leaving for the match or watch party. Do not use a fabricated review
badge, crowd statistic, or club crest without approval. The page should feel
like a useful story first and a product page second.

### Copy

**EDITORIAL NOTE**

The small detail that can make a football shirt feel like yours

**The shirt in the wardrobe was not the problem. It was the feeling that it
could have belonged to anybody.**

Last season, [CUSTOMER NAME] had a familiar matchday routine. The scarf came
out of the drawer. The same jacket went on. The group chat filled with the
usual predictions.

But when the photos were taken, there was nothing in the outfit that carried
the story of the day. No name on the back. No number connected to a player,
child, partner, or memory. Just another shirt among hundreds that looked close
enough to the same.

That is the part most fan merchandise gets wrong. The badge may say which side
you support, but it does not say why this match matters to you.

### The search for something more personal

The obvious options were easy to find. A generic shirt. A novelty slogan. A
cheap printed top that looked good on a product image and less convincing
after a few washes.

Each option solved one small part of the problem. None solved the moment [CUSTOMER
NAME] actually wanted: putting on a shirt that felt made for the occasion,
not pulled from a rack for everybody.

### The simple change

The difference was personalisation.

Instead of starting with a shirt that already had a story attached to it, the
supporter could choose the name and number that mattered to them. A surname.
A child’s number. A favourite player. The year of a first match. The detail is
small, but it changes the emotional weight of the shirt.

That is the idea behind the [PRODUCT NAME]: a custom matchday shirt built around
the detail you want to wear, photograph, and remember.

You choose the available style, enter the name and number, check the preview,
and continue to Shopify checkout. No long questionnaire. No need to explain
why the number matters.

### What you can decide before checkout

- The shirt style: [LIST AVAILABLE STYLES].
- The size: [LIST SIZE RANGE AND FIT NOTE].
- The name: [PERSONALISATION RULE].
- The number: [PERSONALISATION RULE].
- Delivery expectation: [VERIFIED DELIVERY WINDOW].

The details above are shown before payment so you can make a clear decision.
If a size, style, or personalisation option is unavailable, it should be
labelled plainly instead of hidden behind the checkout button.

### The moment it is really for

Imagine opening the package before the next match. The shirt is not just
another colour in the wardrobe. Your name is there. The number is there. When
the group photo happens, the shirt says something without you having to
explain it.

That is the reason to look at the [PRODUCT NAME]. Not because a custom name
magically changes a match. Because the right personal detail can make an
ordinary item feel tied to a real memory.

**CTA: See the shirt, choose my name and number**

The next page shows the available options, the exact price, and the details you
need before the native Shopify checkout.

## Step 1, Variant B: Seven-reasons listicle

### Job in the journey

Give a skimming reader seven concrete reasons to continue. This variant should
make the value easy to understand without pretending that unverified reviews,
scarcity, or performance claims exist.

### Visual job

Use a clean numbered layout with one product image after reason three and a
short sticky CTA on mobile. Keep the list easy to scan. Each reason should
answer one buying question.

### Copy

**7 reasons supporters are choosing a more personal matchday shirt**

The difference between a shirt you own and a shirt you remember is often one
small detail. Here are seven reasons to consider adding your own name and
number to the next one.

**1. It turns a standard shirt into your shirt.**

The same colours can mean something different when the back carries your name,
your family name, or a number with a story behind it.

**2. It gives the gift a personal decision behind it.**

If you are buying for a supporter, you can choose the detail they would never
expect to see waiting for them. Confirm the available personalisation rules
before ordering.

**3. It makes the matchday photo feel less generic.**

The shirt becomes part of the memory instead of disappearing into the crowd.
The product image in this example is a visual placeholder, not proof of a
customer result.

**4. You can check the important details before payment.**

Choose the available style, size, name, and number on the sales page. The final
price, delivery promise, and returns terms must be visible before you continue.

**5. It works for more than one kind of supporter.**

Wear a surname. Mark a special year. Choose a favourite number. Give a child
their first personalised shirt. The right choice depends on the person wearing
it.

**6. It is easier to decide when the offer is clear.**

The next page separates the shirt price, personalisation charge if applicable,
shipping, and any available bundle. No hidden fee should appear for the first
time at checkout.

**7. The best reason is the one only you can explain.**

Maybe it is the name of the person who introduced you to the club. Maybe it is
the number you wore as a child. Maybe it is simply your own name, because you
want one shirt that is unmistakably yours.

**CTA: Show me the available shirts**

Continue to the sales page for the exact options, verified terms, and native
Shopify checkout handoff.

## Step 2, Variant A: Sales page, story and proof

### Job in the journey

Convert interest into a confident product decision. This page should explain
what is being bought, what the buyer controls, what is still subject to store
policy, and why the price is justified.

### Visual job

Show the shirt front and back, a close-up of the personalisation area, a size
guide, and the real product options. Add verified reviews only after they are
provided by the owner. Place the first CTA beside the option selector and
repeat it after objections are answered.

### Copy

**The shirt that carries your matchday detail**

Choose the style, size, name, and number that make this one yours.

You are not buying a vague promise. You are choosing from the available
options, reviewing the personalisation, seeing the exact price, and continuing
to secure the order through Shopify checkout.

**Choose your shirt**

- Style: [AVAILABLE STYLE OPTIONS]
- Size: [SIZE RANGE AND FIT NOTE]
- Name: [CHARACTER LIMIT AND ACCEPTED CHARACTERS]
- Number: [NUMBER RANGE]
- Price: [VERIFIED PRICE]
- Shipping: [VERIFIED SHIPPING TERMS]

**CTA: Choose my shirt and continue**

### Why this feels different

The personalisation is not decoration added after the decision. It is the
decision. It gives the shirt a reason to exist in your wardrobe and a detail to
look for when the package arrives.

### What happens next

1. Select the available shirt and size.
2. Add the name and number, then review the details.
3. Continue to the native Shopify checkout to enter payment and delivery
   information.

The checkout is handled by Shopify. The funnel does not collect card details
and does not change the Shopify checkout screen.

### Proof section

**Use only verified proof here:**

- [VERIFIED CUSTOMER REVIEW WITH PERMISSION]
- [VERIFIED PRODUCT PHOTO]
- [VERIFIED SHIPPING OR PRODUCTION DETAIL]
- [VERIFIED RETURN/PERSONALISATION POLICY]

Until supplied, the page should say: “Real customer photos and reviews will be
added here after owner approval.” It must not fill this section with invented
names, ratings, order counts, or before-and-after claims.

### Questions before you decide

**Can I choose any name and number?**

Use the personalisation rules shown above. If a character, name, or number is
not accepted, the selector should explain that before checkout.

**How do I choose my size?**

Use the published size chart and fit note. If the owner has not confirmed the
measurements, the size guide is not ready for publication.

**When will it arrive?**

Show the verified production and delivery window for the buyer’s destination.
Do not promise a date until the store confirms it.

**Can I return a personalised shirt?**

Show the actual store policy. Personalised products may have different return
terms, so this cannot be guessed in copy.

**Where do I pay?**

Payment and delivery details are entered through Shopify’s native checkout.

**CTA: Review my shirt options**

## Step 2, Variant B: Sales page, offer and value

### Job in the journey

Give a practical buyer a fast, transparent comparison between the cost of the
shirt and the emotional value of owning the personal version. This is not a
discount claim unless the owner supplies a real discount.

### Visual job

Lead with a compact product and price panel, then show a value stack, option
checklist, policy summary, and a single strong CTA. Keep the total price and
shipping visible. A comparison table must use real store data only.

One shirt. Your chosen detail. Clear options before checkout.

**Your order can include:**

## Step 3: Shopify checkout handoff

### Job in the journey

Carry the selected product and personalisation into Shopify’s native checkout
without pretending the funnel owns payment. Confirm the order summary, then
hand off.

### Handoff copy

**Your shirt is ready for checkout**

Before you continue, check:

- Style: [SELECTED STYLE]
- Size: [SELECTED SIZE]
- Name: [SELECTED NAME]
- Number: [SELECTED NUMBER]
- Product total: [VERIFIED TOTAL]
- Shipping: calculated or shown according to the store policy

Shopify will collect your delivery and payment information securely on the
next screen.

**CTA: Continue to secure Shopify checkout**

are governed by the store’s published terms. Replace this example with the

## Tracking and report labels

- `funnel_step_entered`: visitor enters advertorial/listicle, sales, or handoff.
- `funnel_cta_clicked`: visitor clicks the primary continuation CTA.
- `funnel_next_step_entered`: visitor reaches the next funnel step.
- `cart_checkout_started`: observed checkout signal from Shopify/pixel when
  consent and browser conditions allow it.
- `shopify_order_paid`: authoritative paid-order event from the verified
  Shopify webhook.
- Report labels must distinguish observed checkout starts from confirmed paid
  orders and attributed revenue.
